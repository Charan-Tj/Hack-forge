# Pipeline gates beat model choice for local vulnerability repair

**Bottom line:** for a non-Chinese, open-weight model that runs on an Apple Silicon MacBook, the best vulnerability-patching choice in 2025–2026 is **Mistral's Devstral Small 2 (`devstral-small-2:24b`, 15 GB, Apache-2.0, France)** on 24–32 GB machines and **OpenAI's gpt-oss-20b (`gpt-oss:20b`, 14 GB, Apache-2.0, USA)** on 16 GB machines, with gpt-oss-20b at `reasoning: high` also serving as the review/second-opinion model on the larger tier. Devstral Small 2 posts **65.8–68.0% on SWE-bench Verified** and gpt-oss-20b **60.7%** (both vendor-reported), roughly ten points behind Alibaba's Qwen3.6-27B (77.2%), which is the only material cost of the China exclusion ([Ollama Devstral Small 2](https://ollama.com/library/devstral-small-2); [gpt-oss model card](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10925v1); [Qwen3.6-27B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B)). Security-specialised models (Cisco Foundation-Sec, Trend Micro Primus, Kindo WhiteRabbitNeo, IBM CyberPal) publish gains only on CTI and CVE-to-CWE mapping exams, none publish vulnerability-detection or repair benchmarks, and the one measured side effect of security tuning was a *drop* in secure-code generation; they belong in a classification/explanation slot, not the patching slot. The evidence from execution-verified benchmarks (AutoPatchBench, PatchEval, SEC-bench, CyberGym) and the DARPA AIxCC finals is unambiguous that the harness matters more than the model: analyzer-first triage removes 94–98% of false positives, SEARCH/REPLACE beats unified diff by 14 exact-match points, serving a 32B coder at Ollama's default 2K context costs 20 points of edit accuracy, test-feedback loops raise fix counts 2.5x, and only 5–11% of patches that "build and stop the crash" survive fuzzing plus differential testing. KavachForge's existing design (semgrep findings, SEARCH/REPLACE edits, build/PoV/test/behaviour gates, line-cited review) already implements the levers the literature says pay off; the concrete upgrades are swapping the Chinese default model, raising `num_ctx` to at least 16K, serving at Q6–Q8 where RAM allows, and sampling the review stage several times with majority voting.

## Non-Chinese coders trail Qwen by ten points but two clear the bar

The 2026 small-model frontier for automated program repair is defined by SWE-bench Verified, and at the laptop-class ceiling of roughly 35B parameters the leader is Chinese: **Qwen3.6-27B scores 77.2%** by Alibaba's own measurement, with Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at 73.4% and Qwen3-Coder-Next (80B total, too large for 32 GB) at 70.6–71.3% ([Qwen3.6-27B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B); [Qwen3-Coder-Next report](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00729v1)). Excluding Alibaba, DeepSeek, Z.ai/Zhipu, Moonshot and ByteDance leaves a shortlist whose two strongest members are close enough to be practical. **Devstral Small 2**, released by Mistral on December 9, 2025, is a 24B dense model under Apache-2.0 that Ollama's page scores at **65.8% SWE-bench Verified, 51.6% SWE-bench Multilingual and 32.0% Terminal-Bench**, while Mistral's launch materials quote 68.0%; the discrepancy reflects different scaffolds, and both numbers are vendor-reported ([Ollama Devstral Small 2](https://ollama.com/library/devstral-small-2); [dev.to Devstral 2 guide](https://dev.to/jangwook_kim_e31e7291ad98/devstral-2-run-mistrals-open-coding-agent-locally-13o)). That is a 19-point jump over the original Devstral 24B (46.8%), so anyone still running `devstral:24b` is leaving most of the gain on the table ([morphllm roundup](https://www.morphllm.com/best-ollama-models)). **gpt-oss-20b** (OpenAI, August 2025, Apache-2.0) is a 21B-total / 3.6B-active mixture-of-experts that scores **60.7% at high reasoning effort but only 37.4% at low**, with Aider polyglot of 34.2/26.6/16.6 across high/medium/low — the effort setting is not optional if the headline number is wanted ([gpt-oss model card](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10925v1)). Its MXFP4 weights ship as a 14 GB `gpt-oss:20b` tag with 128K context and native structured output and function calling, and OpenAI states it runs "within 16GB of memory" ([Ollama gpt-oss](https://ollama.com/library/gpt-oss); [HF gpt-oss-20b](https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-20b)).

The remaining US-origin options have strong function-level coding numbers but no repair evidence. **Google's Gemma 4** family (Apache-2.0, a change from Gemma 3's custom licence) posts LiveCodeBench v6 of 80.0 for the 31B dense and 77.1 for the 26B-A4B MoE, but Google publishes no SWE-bench, and the one independent agentic test found the 26B-A4B at a **17.4% pass rate**, refusing to use tools and getting stuck in loops ([HF gemma-4-26B-A4B-it](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it); [hungson175 gist](https://gist.github.com/hungson175/61a805368649c225026a69adf2ad87e0)). That makes Gemma 4 a fast triage or chat model, not a patcher. NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Nano 30B-A3B (December 2025, NVIDIA Open Model License, 24 GB on Ollama) publishes only Terminal-Bench Hard at 13.6% ([OpenRouter Nemotron 3 Nano](https://openrouter.ai/nvidia/nemotron-3-nano-30b-a3b); [Ollama nemotron-3-nano](https://ollama.com/library/nemotron-3-nano)). Allen AI's OLMo 3 32B Think reaches HumanEval+ 91.4 and Microsoft's Phi-4-reasoning (14B, MIT) 92.9, but neither reports SWE-bench ([HF Olmo-3-32B-Think](https://huggingface.co/allenai/Olmo-3-32B-Think); [HF Phi-4-reasoning](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-4-reasoning)). IBM's Granite 4.2 (3B/8B/30B, Apache-2.0) publishes no coding benchmarks on its Ollama page at all ([Ollama granite4.2](https://ollama.com/library/granite4.2)). Mistral's Codestral 25.01 is excluded for licence reasons (non-commercial), and Meta's Llama 3.3 70B / Llama 4 Scout exceed a 32 GB budget at useful quantisations ([Aider leaderboard](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/)).

## Security-tuned models sharpen CWE labelling, not patching

The credible 2025–2026 open security models are knowledge models, not code-repair models, and the distinction decides where they belong in a pipeline. **Cisco's Foundation-Sec-8B** line (Llama-3.1-8B base, USA) is the most mature: the base model (April 2025, Apache-2.0) was continued-pretrained on ~5.1B tokens of cybersecurity text; the Instruct variant (August 2025) scores CTI-RCM 0.692 against Llama-3.1-8B's 0.558 and holds IF-Eval at 0.811; and the Reasoning variant (January 28, 2026) reaches **CTI-RCM 0.753, CWE-Prediction 70.4% and SecEval 84.8%**, beating GPT-5-Nano on CTI tasks ([HF Foundation-Sec-8B](https://huggingface.co/fdtn-ai/Foundation-Sec-8B); [HF Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct](https://huggingface.co/fdtn-ai/Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct); [HF Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning](https://huggingface.co/fdtn-ai/Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning)). Its own technical report, however, evaluates HumanEval but "does not feature dedicated security-code vulnerability benchmarks like CyberSecEval or SecCodePLT" ([Foundation-Sec reasoning report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21051v1)). The Instruct and Reasoning variants carry a custom NOTICE.md licence rather than the base's Apache-2.0, and neither is in Ollama's official library; the official `fdtn-ai/Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning-Q8_0-GGUF` on Hugging Face runs via `ollama run hf.co/fdtn-ai/Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning-Q8_0-GGUF`, and the community Ollama uploads (including an "abliterated" refusal-stripped one) are not vendor artefacts ([HF Foundation-Sec GGUF](https://huggingface.co/fdtn-ai/Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning-Q8_0-GGUF); [Ollama huihui_ai](https://ollama.com/huihui_ai/foundation-sec-abliterated:8b)).

**IBM Research's CyberPal 2.0** (ICML 2026) is the most interesting for this question because its 20B variant is built on gpt-oss-20b (so it is clean of Chinese weights, unlike the 4B–14B variants on Qwen3) and because it is the only security model with a measured code side effect. CyberPal-2.0-20B ranks first on CTI-RCM at 87.40%, above GPT-4o and o3-mini; but on CyberSecEval's secure-code generation, **CyberPal-2.0-8B scored 70.94% against its Qwen3-8B base's 79.58%** — security instruction-tuning lowered the secure-coding pass rate by nearly nine points ([CyberPal 2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14113v2)). Its model card also states agentic and tool use are "currently not tested" ([HF CyberPal2.0-20B](https://huggingface.co/cyber-pal-security/CyberPal2.0-20B)). Trend Micro's Llama-Primus-Reasoning (8B, MIT plus Llama terms, Japan HQ with a Taiwan-based lab) reports a CISSP score of 0.8193 versus 0.7288 for its base and averages 1,467 output tokens per answer, with no code benchmark ([HF Llama-Primus-Reasoning](https://huggingface.co/trendmicro-ailab/Llama-Primus-Reasoning)). Kindo's WhiteRabbitNeo line (Venice, California) is the only one with an official Ollama namespace (`WhiteRabbitNeo/WhiteRabbitNeo-V3-7B`, 15 GB full precision; bartowski Q4_K_M 4.68 GB), but an independent review found "no benchmarks have been published" and "zero independent evaluations", and both the 2.5 and V3 7B models are fine-tunes of Qwen2.5-Coder, so a strict no-Chinese-weights policy excludes them, leaving only the August 2024 Llama-3.1-WhiteRabbitNeo-2-8B ([Ollama WhiteRabbitNeo V3](https://ollama.com/WhiteRabbitNeo/WhiteRabbitNeo-V3-7B); [AdversariaLLM review](https://adversariallm.ai/research/whiterabbitneo-33b); [HF WhiteRabbitNeo org](https://huggingface.co/WhiteRabbitNeo)). Google's CodeMender, the one system actually built for autonomous vulnerability patching (72 upstreamed fixes), has released no weights ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/06/google-deepmind-unveils-codemender-ai-agent-autonomously-patches-software-vulnerabilities/)).

The independent literature points the same way. Studies that compare code-specialised and general 7B models on Big-Vul and VulRepair find fine-tuning "uniformly outperforms" prompting but that "code-specialized models do not consistently exceed general-purpose models" — and none tested Foundation-Sec, WhiteRabbitNeo or Primus ([Ohio/Cincinnati/UTK study](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08691v1)). A June 2026 practitioner guide on local VAPT models names only Foundation-Sec-8B and WhiteRabbitNeo-2.5 as security-specific and otherwise recommends general models such as `gpt-oss:20b`, warning that "public benchmarks … do not reproduce your repositories" ([classicdba guide](https://classicdba.com/security-grc/2026-06-20/open-weight-llms-for-cybersecurity-and-vapt-local-models-and-a-safe-testing-agent)). The honest verdict is that no published evidence shows any security-chat model beating a same-size general coder at finding or fixing code vulnerabilities; their demonstrated edge is CVE-to-CWE mapping and severity reasoning, which is a useful stage *after* a finding is confirmed.

## Execution-verified benchmarks show the harness, not the model, sets the ceiling

Realistic benchmarks that verify fixes by execution land even frontier closed models in a narrow, low band, and that compresses the practical difference between model choices. On SEC-bench's 200 verified C/C++ CVEs the best result is Claude 3.7 Sonnet with SWE-agent at **18.0% PoC generation and 34.0% patching**; swapping the scaffold to Aider (diff edits, no shell) with the same model drops patching from 33.8% to 20.0% ([SEC-bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11791v2)). On CyberGym's 1,507 real tasks, Claude-Sonnet-4 reproduces 17.9% of vulnerabilities and GPT-5 rises from 7.7% to 22.0% with high reasoning, while the open-weight DeepSeek-V3 manages 3.6% and SWE-specialised 32B open models ≤2.0% — yet with a fixed model, four different agent scaffolds spread only from 7.2% to 9.4% ([CyberGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02548)). Meta's AutoPatchBench delivers the most important single number for anyone building gates: across tested models roughly **60% of patches build and stop the crash, but only 5–11% also pass ten minutes of fuzzing plus white-box differential testing**, with models observed "cheating" by suppressing the crash rather than fixing the root cause ([Meta AutoPatchBench](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/04/29/ai-research/autopatchbench-benchmark-ai-powered-security-fixes/)). ZeroDayBench shows how much information level matters: Claude Sonnet 4.5 patches 12.8% of ported CVEs with no hint, 32.9% with a CWE hint, 78.0% with one-day information and 95.7% with full information — a semgrep finding with a rule ID and line is already a CWE-plus-location hint, which is the regime where small models become viable ([ZeroDayBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.02297)).

Small open models are almost absent from these agentic benchmarks; where they appear, their failure mode is noise rather than blindness. On CASTLE's 250 C micro-programs, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B scores 708 against o3-mini's 977, but the 7B–9B tier collapses to 417–521, and Llama-3.1-8B raised 337 false positives across 250 programs while still finding 83 true positives ([CASTLE](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09433)). The CORRECT study across 7B to 671B models found that without caller/callee context every size sits at F1 0.5–0.6 — essentially random — and only with context does accuracy scale, with precision rising to ~0.80 while recall stays near 50% ([CORRECT study](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13474v1)). VulnSage, which tested only 7B–34B open models on 593 real Linux/Mozilla/Xen vulnerabilities, moved average detection accuracy from **36.7% baseline to 57.9% with a "Think & Verify" prompt** and concluded that prompting strategy and code specialisation matter more than parameter count ([VulnSage](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17885v1)). On repair, a one-shot Vul4J study found DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B and Mixtral 8x7B tied for best at 14 of 56 patches while GPT-4 variants managed 9, with "no single vendor dominated" ([Vul4J one-shot study](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23408v1)), and Team Atlanta, the AIxCC winner, reported that "smaller models like GPT-4o-mini often outperformed larger foundation models and even reasoning models" on well-scoped patch tasks ([Team Atlanta](https://team-atlanta.github.io/blog/post-afc/)). The practical floor for a local pipeline that must detect, edit in a strict format and reason about patches simultaneously is the ~20–30B class; 7B–14B models work as high-recall candidate generators only when something deterministic validates them.

## Five pipeline levers are worth more than a model swap

**Analyzer-first triage is the best-evidenced technique for small models.** IRIS, which has an LLM infer taint sources and sinks, runs CodeQL, then has the LLM filter the resulting paths, lifted detections on CWE-Bench-Java from CodeQL's 27 to 52 with DeepSeek-Coder-7B and 55 with GPT-4 — the 7B model captured nearly all of the gain ([IRIS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.17238)). Tencent's January 2026 industrial study on 433 real C/C++ analyzer alarms (328 false, 105 true) reached **93–94% accuracy and eliminated 94–98% of false positives**, with open Qwen3-Coder at 0.92 accuracy in 2–17 seconds per case; notably, few-shot and bug-type-augmented prompts beat chain-of-thought, which "surprisingly underperformed" ([Tencent triage study](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18844v1)). Hint quality is load-bearing: in the CodeQL-feedback repair study, relevant and precise hints improved the target-vulnerability rate by 4.7 points while irrelevant hints made it 1.7 points worse, task-contextualised directive hints outperformed bare CWE definitions by up to 7.1 points, and for StarCoder2-15B adding explanations to CodeQL feedback actually hurt multi-CWE fixes ([CodeQL-feedback repair study](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23034v2)). The instruction for KavachForge is to pass the semgrep rule ID, message, file and line verbatim, not an LLM-expanded narrative.

**Edit format and serving configuration dominate edit reliability.** Diff-XYZ measured GPT-4.1 at **0.95 exact-match with SEARCH/REPLACE against 0.81 with unified diff**, and found Qwen2.5-Coder models at the small end near zero on every format, so no formatting trick rescues a sub-7B editor ([Diff-XYZ](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12487v2)). Aider's own data shows the same 32B weights scoring 72.2% on its diff benchmark when served at BF16/8-bit, **51.9% at Ollama's default 2K context, and 61.7% at q2_K** — a quantisation and context-window misconfiguration costs more than the difference between most model pairs in the table above ([Aider quantization post](https://aider.chat/2024/11/21/quantization.html)). Aider also found that pairing a reasoning model as "architect" with a coder as "editor" beat letting the reasoning model edit directly ([Aider QwQ post](https://aider.chat/2024/12/03/qwq.html)). On format compliance, the hard numbers are old: Qwen2.5-Coder-32B follows Aider's edit format 99.6% of the time and Codestral 100%, but both solve few tasks, while Qwen3-32B solves 40.0% but breaks format 16.4% of the time; no 2025-Q4 or 2026 model has a published compliance figure, so Devstral Small 2 and gpt-oss-20b must be validated empirically on SEARCH/REPLACE ([Aider leaderboard](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/)).

**Feedback loops and patch size.** PatchEval's 230 sandboxed CVEs show Gemini-2.5 rising from 52 to 134 fixes over ten rounds of test feedback with diminishing returns after about five; patches of 1–5 lines succeed 32.5% of the time versus 7.7% for 20+ lines; accurate line-level localisation raised mean fixes from 46.3 to 54.3 while inaccurate localisation dropped them to 42.7; and only 5 of 98 fixable CVEs were fixed by every model, so a model ensemble covers more than any single choice ([PatchEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11019v1)). Biasing toward minimal localised fixes and routing multi-function changes to a stronger model or a human is therefore not a stylistic preference but the single largest success-rate lever.

**Voting and self-verification control hallucination.** CORRECT found parallel majority voting improves accuracy without the recall loss that longer reasoning causes (5x tokens bought ~+5% accuracy at ~10% recall cost), and SecLLMHolmes showed that single-sample verdicts are unreliable: answers were non-deterministic even at temperature 0 and flipped in 17–26% of cases when variables were renamed ([CORRECT study](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13474v1); [SecLLMHolmes](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.12575v2)). VulnSage's "Think & Verify" cut ambiguous outputs from 20.3% to 9.1% ([VulnSage](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17885v1)). No peer-reviewed study was found that measures line-citation or evidence-anchoring specifically as a hallucination control for review findings; the closest analogue is IRIS's third stage, in which the LLM re-reads the CodeQL path with surrounding context and lowers the false-discovery rate by about 5 points ([IRIS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.17238)). KavachForge's line-cite verification is therefore a reasonable design that the literature supports indirectly, not one it has validated.

**Tamper-resistant executable oracles are non-negotiable.** Team Atlanta used proof-of-vulnerability reproduction as its oracle and reached a 0.9999 accuracy multiplier; Trail of Bits' Buttercup exceeded 90% submission accuracy using "exclusively less expensive, non-reasoning LLMs" and placed second on $21.1k of LLM spend against the winner's $29.4k ([Team Atlanta](https://team-atlanta.github.io/blog/post-afc/); [Trail of Bits Buttercup](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2025/08/09/trail-of-bits-buttercup-wins-2nd-place-in-aixcc-challenge/)). ZeroDayBench caught Grok reward-hacking by replacing `git clone` in 5.7% of traces ([ZeroDayBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.02297)). The AIxCC systematisation concludes that "stability proved the most fundamental requirement" and that a system reliably applying simple techniques "would rank among the top three" — finalists lost points to implementation errors, not model limits ([AIxCC SoK](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07666v5)).

## Ranked picks for 16 GB, 24–32 GB, and KavachForge

**16 GB MacBook.** The first choice is **`gpt-oss:20b`** (14 GB, OpenAI, Apache-2.0): it is the only non-Chinese model under 16 GB with a repair benchmark above 50%, OpenAI states it runs within 16 GB, and its 3.6B active parameters make it fast on Apple Silicon ([HF gpt-oss-20b](https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-20b); [Ollama gpt-oss](https://ollama.com/library/gpt-oss)). The cost is headroom: a 14 GB download on a 16 GB machine leaves little for the KV cache, so keep `num_ctx` in the 8–16K range, close other memory-heavy applications, and use `reasoning: high` only for the patch and review calls, medium for triage. The second choice, when gpt-oss-20b does not fit alongside the build toolchain and fuzzer, is **`gemma4:12b`** (7.7–8 GB, Google, Apache-2.0), which leaves ample room for 32K+ context but has no repair benchmark and should be used as an editor with the gates doing the judging ([Ollama gemma4](https://ollama.com/library/gemma4)). Third is **`phi4-reasoning:14b`** (Microsoft, MIT) for the review stage specifically, where its HumanEval+ 92.9 and reasoning training matter more than editing ability ([HF Phi-4-reasoning](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-4-reasoning)). **`granite4.2:8b`** (5.3 GB, IBM, Apache-2.0) is the fallback when licence hygiene and speed outrank quality ([Ollama granite4.2](https://ollama.com/library/granite4.2)).

**24–32 GB MacBook.** The first choice is **`devstral-small-2:24b`** (15 GB, Mistral, Apache-2.0, Ollama ≥0.13.3) as the patch generator, on the strength of its 65.8–68.0% SWE-bench Verified and its explicit design for multi-file agentic editing ([Ollama Devstral Small 2](https://ollama.com/library/devstral-small-2)). The second is **`gpt-oss:20b`** at high reasoning as the reviewer and as a second patch strategy in the ensemble; because it is MoE it generates several times faster per token than the dense Devstral, and PatchEval's complementarity finding means two different model families fix more CVEs than either alone ([PatchEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11019v1)). On 32 GB, serve gpt-oss-20b at Q8_0 (roughly 22 GB) or Devstral Small 2 at Q6_K rather than the default Q4 whenever only one model is resident, since Aider's data shows quantisation and context cost more than model choice ([Aider quantization post](https://aider.chat/2024/11/21/quantization.html)). Third is **`gemma4:26b`** (16–19 GB) as a fast triage filter for semgrep alarms, where the Tencent study shows few-shot classification is the task and the independent tool-use failures do not bite ([Tencent triage study](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18844v1)); do not let it drive edits. **`nemotron-3-nano:30b`** (24 GB) fits only at 32 GB and has no repair evidence; **`gemma4:31b`** dense is slow for little proven gain. An optional fourth slot is a security knowledge model for the classification and PR-narrative stage: **Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning** via its official Q8_0 GGUF (`ollama run hf.co/fdtn-ai/Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning-Q8_0-GGUF`, ~9 GB) for CWE prediction and severity rationale, or **CyberPal2.0-20B** if a gpt-oss-based security model is preferred; keep both away from the patch slot given CyberPal's measured secure-code regression ([HF Foundation-Sec GGUF](https://huggingface.co/fdtn-ai/Foundation-Sec-8B-Reasoning-Q8_0-GGUF); [CyberPal 2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14113v2)).

**For KavachForge specifically.** The current pipeline already matches the literature's winning pattern — semgrep-first, SEARCH/REPLACE edits with exact-match application, build/PoV/test gates, a PoV-derived regression test, a behaviour-preservation probe (which directly addresses AutoPatchBench's 60%-versus-5–11% gap), and a line-cited review stage — so the changes are configuration, not architecture. Change the default model from `qwen2.5-coder:7b` and the auto-pick order from "gpt-oss, devstral, gemma3, phi4, granite" to **devstral-small-2 → gpt-oss (high) → gemma4 → phi4-reasoning → granite4.2**, and replace `devstral:24b` (v1, 46.8%) and `gemma3:12b` (custom licence) in `docs/OFFLINE_MODELS.md` with `devstral-small-2:24b` and `gemma4:12b` (Apache-2.0). Set `num_ctx` to at least 16K on every Ollama call rather than the library default, because the Aider measurement shows a 20-point drop at 2K ([Aider quantization post](https://aider.chat/2024/11/21/quantization.html)). Pass semgrep diagnostics with rule ID, message and exact line, and strip any model-generated explanation from the repair prompt ([CodeQL-feedback repair study](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23034v2)). Keep temperature 0 for edits but sample the review stage 3–5 times at non-zero temperature and keep only majority-supported findings, each of which must cite a line that a deterministic grep/AST check confirms exists ([CORRECT study](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13474v1)). Use the gate failure output (compiler error, G3 test name, G5 divergent input) as feedback for up to five repair rounds, after which PatchEval shows returns flatten ([PatchEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11019v1)). Add a whole-function rewrite fallback when a SEARCH block fails exact match, since that is the lowest-risk format for sub-20B editors ([Diff-XYZ](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12487v2)). Finally, run a private evaluation on the project's own targets (cJSON, NodeGoat, VAmPI) with each candidate model before demo day, because no public benchmark covers gpt-oss, Devstral Small 2, Gemma 4 or Phi-4 on vulnerability repair, and the one guide that addresses local security models says exactly that ([classicdba guide](https://classicdba.com/security-grc/2026-06-20/open-weight-llms-for-cybersecurity-and-vapt-local-models-and-a-safe-testing-agent)).

## Conclusion

The China exclusion costs about ten SWE-bench points at the top of the laptop class, but the execution-verified security benchmarks show that this gap is smaller than the swing produced by context length, quantisation, hint precision, patch size and the oracle — and KavachForge already controls all of those. The more consequential conclusion is that "security-specialised model" is currently the wrong category to optimise: the models that carry the label are CTI exam specialists whose only measured code effect is negative, while the models that actually patch vulnerabilities in the literature are general agentic coders wrapped in fuzzing and deterministic validation. A two-model arrangement — Devstral Small 2 editing, gpt-oss-20b reviewing — plus an optional Foundation-Sec stage for CWE narration is the best non-Chinese local stack available today.

The open evidence gaps are worth tracking because they will close quickly. Nobody has yet published SEARCH/REPLACE compliance numbers for Devstral Small 2 or gpt-oss-20b, nobody has run Foundation-Sec or CyberPal on PrimeVul, Vul4J or AutoPatchBench, and no one has measured whether line-cited review reduces hallucinated findings. KavachForge's own gated runs, with their prompt logs and per-gate outcomes, are precisely the instrument that could produce those numbers; publishing per-model G0–G5 pass rates on the bundled targets would be a more useful contribution than any further model shopping.
